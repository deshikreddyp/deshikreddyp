Ph.D. research

Intrathecal drug transport and injection-protocol optimization

Gomez Lab, Purdue University · Feb. 2025 – present

  • Developed a multicomponent finite-element solver for intrathecal drug transport, with SUPG stabilization and a custom discontinuity-capturing operator for the advection-dominated problem.
  • Adapted the coupled-momentum method for small-deformation spinal FSI to avoid ALE mesh motion, reducing computational cost by approximately 30× and enabling simulations over long time scales.
  • Quantified how injection-induced CSF pressure dynamics, drug solution baricity, gravity, and body posture affect intrathecal drug distribution.
  • Developed a reduced-order transport model that cuts compute time for a 1-hour drug-transport prediction from 20 hours to 1 second.
  • Enabled in silico evaluation of AAV injection protocols for ALS to inform the design of nonhuman primate trials.

Mixed-dimensional fluid–structure interaction model for CSF flow in the spinal canal

Gomez Lab, Purdue University · May 2023 – Jan. 2025

Publication: Putluru, D.R., Tepole, A.B. & Gomez, H. Mixed-dimensional fluid–structure interaction simulations reveal key mechanisms of cerebrospinal fluid dynamics in the spinal canal. Fluids and Barriers of the CNS 22, 81 (2025). DOI link

Cerebrospinal fluid flow dynamics plays a critical role in structural disorders of the central nervous system and in the design of effective procedures for intrathecal drug delivery. Medical imaging has only partially characterized CSF dynamics, while computational models can provide a high-resolution description and improve mechanistic understanding.

Many anatomically accurate computational models of CSF dynamics in the spinal canal ignore tissue compliance, even though compliance is important for capturing pulse wave velocity and the craniocaudal decay of CSF pulsations.

This work developed a mixed-dimensional fluid–structure interaction method for high-fidelity simulation of CSF dynamics in anatomically accurate spinal canal models while accounting for compliance effects from the dura mater and epidural fat. The approach bypasses a computational bottleneck associated with the multiscale geometry of spinal tissues, enabling full-length patient-specific simulations at approximately 60× lower cost than fully volumetric FSI.

  • Implemented residual-based VMS stabilization and a custom Newton–Krylov solver in PETSc.
  • Formulated a deformation-Jacobian-based stiffening model for ALE mesh motion that suppresses element inversion under large deformation and near-contact.
  • Developed a custom preconditioner that is 3× faster than PETSc's GAMG on the mixed-dimensional FSI problem.

Undergraduate research and coursework

Numerical and experimental analysis of a Savonius-style wind turbine at low wind speeds

Bachelor's thesis, IIT Roorkee · Advisor: Prof. Sushanta Dutta

The performance of a Savonius-style wind turbine depends on overlap ratio and blade shape characteristics. This project optimized geometric parameters of the blade design, including aspect ratios, blade profile, and overlap distance, to improve the coefficient of moment Cm.

Transient film boiling around a hot sphere using the volume-of-fluid method

Flow Physics Laboratory, IIT Roorkee · Jul. – Dec. 2021

Developed an OpenFOAM finite-volume solver using the volume-of-fluid method and phase-change physics to simulate transient film boiling around a heated sphere (diameter 6.35 mm, 1063 K) in a quiescent water pool. Analyzed vapor-film evolution and validated Nusselt number and heat-transfer coefficient against experimental measurements and analytical benchmarks.

Shock-wave propagation through a Y-split bifurcation

Compressible Flow course project, IIT Roorkee · Aug. – Nov. 2021

Recreated a shock-tube bifurcation experiment in OpenFOAM v6 using the shock-capturing rhoCentralFoam solver, and validated pressure and flow predictions against measurements. A parametric study of the branch angle quantified its effect on junction overpressure and flow structures, informing tunnel designs that limit damage from blast-induced waves.

Hybrid finite-element method for guided-wave propagation in a 3D elbow joint

Undergraduate research, IIT Roorkee · Feb. – May 2021

Developed a hybrid semi-analytical finite-element (SAFE) framework in MATLAB and COMSOL to characterize Lamb-wave propagation through elbow pipes, targeting detection of crack-induced wave anomalies in large, hard-to-inspect pipelines. A 3-inch Schedule 40 steel elbow (bend radius 133.35 mm, 90° bend) with semi-infinite extensions was modeled by coupling the 3D FE bend to 2D SAFE models through extracted mass and stiffness matrices and node-wise matching of end-face degrees of freedom.

H2S dispersion cloud modeling in FLACS and OpenFOAM

Modeled the dispersion of an H2S gas cloud from a leak inside an open plant. Simulations were run in FLACS and in OpenFOAM, first with reactingFoam and then with PDRFoam and a buoyancy solver.

Failure analysis of a wind-turbine blade at high wind speeds

Team lead, course project, IIT Roorkee · Autumn 2019

Studied material selection and failure of a wind-turbine blade and shaft at critical wind speeds using ANSYS Fluent, Structural, and fatigue analyses.
